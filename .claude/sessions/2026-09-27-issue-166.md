# Session: Issue #166 Scoped LAT Parsing (ACTIVE)

## Problem

Large Acts are often relevant only in part (e.g. the Companies Act 2006 for environmental reporting: `part/15/chapter/5` is 92KB against a 13.5MB whole body). Legal keeps no record of a law's parse scope, so every LAT parse fetches the whole body, and a law parsed partially by hand would silently widen on the next re-parse. (UK_ukpga_2003_21, the Communications Act, is a whole-body parse: Parts 1–6, 10,900 rows, no schedules.)

## Enabling-extent use (2026-09-29)

Jason: "I'd rather we had the data we do need in LAT rather than caches or off db stores."

Tier 1 of the live status work found **89 non-Making parent Acts without LAT** cited by the SIs' enabling provisions. Examples: European Communities Act 1972 (125 SIs), Road Traffic Regulation Act 1984 (29), Social Security Act 1990 (27), Finance Acts, Scotland Act 1998.

Parsing a whole non-Making Act only to discard it (lean LAT) would lose the section extents again. So a scoped LAT holds the cited sections only:
- a section fragment's `data.xml` carries the section and its enclosing Part with that Part's RestrictExtent, so the LAT parser's (now inherited) extent_code is correct;
- ECA 1972 s.2(2) gives a sourced UK extent, not a legacy one.

Fractalaw must know such LAT is partial and extent-only (manifest scope/coverage), and should not treat it as the law's whole text.

## Build notes (2026-09-29)

- **Fragment parse ≡ full parse.** WRA 1991 `section/82` gives the same section_ids, sort-key structure and (inherited) extent as the full body. Only positions differ (1… vs 1158…), so widening a scope later changes positions and sort keys, not ids: a structure-only update for fractalaw.
- Annotation ids are numbered per document, so they collided across fragments; they are now merged and renumbered.
- Tier 1 dry run (`mix lat.scope --enabling --batch 1a`): 19 non-Making parents to scope, each with a few sections, e.g. ECA 1972 s.2, Local Government Act 1972 s.265, Scotland Act 1998 s.88.
