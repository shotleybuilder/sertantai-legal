# Session: Commencement Ruleset — Meta (ACTIVE)

## Problem

Four readers answer "when did provision P of law L come into force, and where?", each from source XML and each covering a different subset: `LawStart` + `Clause` (a labelled law's start, 14 treatments, LR-33), `LawStart.Provisions` (its own clause's provision dates, LR-34), `AmendingLaw.Commencement` (an amending provision's own clause, LR-08) and `CommencingOrder` (hop 2: the orders a note names). The amending changes they can't date are now the largest fillable part of A8's undated-change class: 2,552 undated amending-law records (LR-44: 1,463 Acts whose held commencement orders yield no date, 706 Acts with no order named, 383 SIs whose own start isn't read); J6's 18,394 `commenced_by_order` rows wait for the same order reading.

Jason 2026-10-18: "The key would be the schema for the abstracted rules. Rather than working with the source XML the amending commencing text is translated to a common ruleset that's easy to read."

Design draft: `lat-refactor/2026-10-18-lr-44-amending-commencement.md` (schema, examples, evaluator). Gemini review: `backend/data/code-reviews/2026-10-18-commencement-ruleset-schema-gemini.md`.

## Architecture

- **Readers** translate source text into rules, once per source document (a law's own commencement clause; each commencement order; an amending law's clause). All XML and prose handling lives there.
- **The ruleset**: one rule per commencement statement — `commences`, `scope`, `when`, `territory`, `purposes`, `source` (law, provision, sentence verbatim), `treatment` — stored and printable (`mix lat.commencement --law X`).
- **One evaluator** (pure) dates any provision from the rules only, for every consumer: the labelled law's start and its rows (LR-33/34), an amending change's date (LR-08), J6's by-order rows.

## Gemini review (2026-10-18): what the schema gains

- **Fields to add**: retrospective effect ("is deemed to have come into force on …"); expiry ("ceases to have effect …", sunset); `when: on_event` ("on the day the Treaty enters into force for the UK"); scope modifier `not_already_in_force` ("so far as not already in force").
- **Reader duties**: a Schedule is commenced through the section introducing it ("Schedule 5 has effect" in s.42); scope refs to subsection / paragraph level.
- **`purposes`: a fixed set** with the verbatim words in `source` — `nil` (full), `for_making_secondary_legislation`, `in_relation_to`, `for_specified_purposes` (any non-nil value is a partial entry).
- **Evaluator flaw** in the draft: "most specific wins" would keep s.50 partly in force forever when Order 1 commences it "for the purpose of making regulations" and Order 2 commences "the remaining provisions". Fix: collect every rule covering the provision, apply in date order, a full commencement supersedes earlier partial ones.

## Decided (Jason 2026-10-18: "Adopt the 4 purposes and scope to bringing into force. The abstraction should simplify not complicate")

- **Bringing into force only.** Expiry / sunset is out (an ending, the change register's like a repeal). No retrospective flag: "deemed to have come into force on 1 April" is that date.
- **`purposes`**: Gemini's four — `nil` (fully), `for_making_secondary_legislation`, `in_relation_to`, `for_specified_purposes`; the words verbatim in `source`.
- **`when`**: `date` · `after` (made / passed + offset) · `named_day` · `appointed` (a day fixed later: by order, notice or event — undated until a rule dates it) · `with` (another provision's commencement) · `unread`.
- **No scope modifier** for "so far as not already in force": the evaluator applies rules in date order and a full commencement supersedes earlier partial ones, which is what the words mean.
