## Design (2026-10-19/20)

**Row-free reading.** `Target` asked "does this instruction name *this* row?". `Amendment.Reader` reads an amending provision once against the candidate laws its notes point to (one hop) and expands the targets `Target` would accept: the law named (nearest text: instruction, context, container title, text before — LR-41); quoted paths (a provision's, the named brackets then the path, paths numbered from the substituted subsection — LR-43 — or from the provision holding a replaced bracket); a named provision with no brackets (whole), else every in-order combination of the brackets named; an insertion only what it quotes or names as inserted; a quote's own provision never as a named one; repeals-table lines. `AmendingLaw.Target` / `RepealTable` / the collector stay untouched (LabelVersion inputs) until AR-04 deletes them.

**Same records.** `Amendment.Collect` reproduces `ChangeCollector.AmendingLaw`'s records (evidence, operation, effect type, dating via `Commencement.Amending`, repeals tables dated by their own entry first — CR-08). Tested on the LR-08 fixtures: same records from the document and from the store.

## The first parity run, and the refactor (2026-10-20)

- A scratch script running the old and new collectors 5 laws at once, each holding every amending law's parsed CLML (xmerl: one law's peak up to ~3.3 GB), **exhausted system memory and crashed the machine**; it wrote only at the end, so nothing was saved. Rewritten one law at a time (own process, written and `.done`-marked per law, memory per law): safe, but slow — the cost is XML: every law re-parses every amending law its notes point to (EPA 1990 hundreds of times), and both collectors ran.
- From its first 186 laws (1,137 same): 105 old-only (words-level changes on a provision named without brackets, note on a child row — evaluator fixed: words-level lands on the named provision as whole provisions do) and 5 new-only (Clean Air Act 1968 s.5(1) "for paragraph (b) substitute— (b) (c)": reader now reaches s.7(1)(c)).
