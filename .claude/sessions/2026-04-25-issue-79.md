### Model B decision: two independent family signals

**Decision**: `family` and `enacted_families` are semantically independent columns.

| Column | Meaning | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| `family` | What is this law *about*? (intrinsic subject) | SI code mapping + title keywords |
| `enacted_families` | What families does it *enable*? (extrinsic, derived) | Aggregated from child law families |

**Why**: Acts fall into three tiers by enacted_families breadth:
- 370 single-family specialists (e.g. Animal Health Act → 228 children, all ANIMALS)
- 145 domain-anchored (2-5 families, dominant at 60-70%+, e.g. HSWA → OH&S 65%)
- 23 general-purpose enablers (6+ families, e.g. European Communities Act → 46 families, 1241 children)

Forcing `family` to match `enacted_families` is wrong — general enablers (ECA, EU Withdrawal Act)
are correctly `🖤 No Family` because they're constitutional machinery, not domain law.
Domain Acts like EPA are correctly `ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION` even though they also enable WASTE, WATER etc.

**Rejected alternatives**:
- Model A (strip family, use enacted_families only): loses intrinsic subject identity, breaks filtering
- Model C (auto-promote dominant enacted family): circular — child families determine parent, parent used to infer children

**Secondary laws that enact (110 parents)**:
- 54 are NI Orders in Council (_nisi_) — effectively primary legislation for Northern Ireland.
  Behave like Acts: domain-specific, highly coherent (94/110 have single-family children).
- 48 are UK SIs (_uksi_) — unusual. One major outlier: EC (Designation) Order 1972 (12 families,
  183 children) mirrors ECA as a general enabler. The rest are domain-specific.
- Should be treated with higher suspicion in QA — a true SI-enacts-SI is rare and may indicate
  a wrong enacted_by link. When correct, expect tight family coherence.

**SI code as cross-validation signal**:
- Parent's OWN si_code (not enacted_si_codes which is derived from children) provides an
  independent signal. 69% of parents have no usable own si_code (NULL, empty, or `["[]"]`).
- Where it exists: models.ex maps SI codes → families. This can cross-validate the parent's
  `family` assignment independently of enacted_families.
- For children: child SI code can be mapped to family via models.ex and compared against
  the child's assigned `family` — a mismatch there is a real signal of wrong family assignment.
- Three-way cross-check possible: child.family vs models(child.si_code) vs parent.enacted_families
  distribution — disagreement on any axis is worth flagging.

**QA implications for Enacted By tab**: The old mismatch concept (child.family ≠ parent.family)
is meaningless under Model B. `enacted_families` is derived FROM child families, so a child's
family is always present by definition. The real QA questions become:

1. **Link validity**: Is this enacted_by link correct?
   - Secondary (SI) parents are higher suspicion than primary (Act) parents
   - NI Orders (_nisi_) are expected; other SI types less so
   - Re-parse button lets operator verify by re-extracting from legislation.gov.uk XML

2. **Outlier within enacted_families distribution**: Does this child's family stand out
   against siblings? e.g. 1 TRANSPORT child among 236 AGRICULTURE children is suspicious.
   Could be wrong enacted_by link OR wrong child family assignment.

3. **SI code cross-validation**: Does models.ex(child.si_code) agree with child.family?
   If not, either the SI code mapping or the family assignment is wrong — independent of
   the enacted_by relationship.

4. **Unclassified children**: Parents with children but empty enacted_families (207 parents)
   — the children lack family assignment, so the aggregate can't populate.
