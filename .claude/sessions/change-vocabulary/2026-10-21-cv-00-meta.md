# Session: Change Vocabulary — Meta (ACTIVE)

## Problem

Jason 2026-10-21: "Abstracting all change sources into a common vocabulary that we can just 'read' is the only way to do the labelling reliably." Today a change to a provision is known from five sources: CLML markup (Repeal/Addition/Substitution, Extent, RetainText, ChangeId), the changes feed's effects, the amending law's text, commencement (own clause, orders, feed), and notes (pointers only). They are merged twice, by different code: the parse (`EffectIndex`, `EffectTerritory`'s claim order, rule 3, orphan joins) gives each row's text, `excluded_extent` and `markup_status`; the register (`ChangeCollector` → `lat_change_log` → `LabelProjection`) gives the dates and periods, with today's territory taken from the parse (LR-26 D1′). The two merges hold pairwise reconciling rules (rule 3's gate, the claim's step order, J2 "markup wins today", J5) and can disagree. V1-04's territory audit found two such cases: Coronavirus Act 2020 s.78 and SI 2016/1105 reg.72(2)(b). Each fix so far has added another rule at an edge ("we've again slipped into chasing edges in code rather than abstracting").

The commencement ruleset already has the shape: several readers (`OwnClause`, `Order`, `Commencement.Effects`) write one `Commencement.Rule`, and one evaluator dates them. The amendment ruleset has one reader: `Amendment.Reader` reads the amending law's text. AR-00's design review was right for its scope, which was replacing the text matcher for amending instructions; the feed and the markup were never in it. `Amendment.Rule` has no territory: it is added afterwards, one way for register records (`Collect.territory`) and another in the parse.

## Goal

One vocabulary for a change, written by a reader per source and merged by identity (the effect id joins markup, feed and amending law). Each attribute (operation, words or whole provision, targets, territory, date, in force, savings) carries its evidence, and one stated evidence order per attribute replaces the pairwise rules. The parse's territory, the register and the labels all read the merged changes. The parse keeps only what only it can do: placing changed words in the text.

**Simplify (Jason 2026-10-21, on CV-01's inventory):** "lots of refactoring to keep things simple. Stopping all this duplication that's happened." Each attribute is decided once, by one reader per source and one evidence order. The duplicated vocabularies go: six operation verb lists, three qualification parsers, three ways of choosing a date, three range expansions, two type-to-territory maps. So do the stand-in rules for the missing identity (rule 3's gate, AR-04's compensators, J2, T1's blanking).

**Source authority (Jason 2026-10-21):** "The actual legal text is primary. These other sources are derived and secondary."
- **Tier 1, primary:** the legal text. The amending law's instruction, its extent and application clauses, its commencement provisions and orders, and the amended law's own extent and application clauses (C6/C11).
- **Tier 2, derived:** legislation.gov.uk's revised-text markup.
- **Tier 3, derived:** the changes feed.
- **Notes** are pointers only.

Decided:
- **The primary text wins** for every attribute our readers read from it. Derived sources fill only where the primary text isn't held or wasn't read. Every primary–derived disagreement goes to a conflict report, never silently reconciled ("which is probably how leg.gov.uk build their change feed and we will likely have most labels set that way").
- **Every label value carries its tier** (primary / derived), so consumers see which facts rest on legislation.gov.uk's editorial work.
